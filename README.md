# Customer Churn Analysis Using Microsoft Excel

## Project Overview

This project explores customer churn patterns using a dataset containing approximately 64,000 customer records.

The objective is to investigate how churn varies across customer segments and identify patterns that could help a subscription-based business understand customer retention.

The analysis was conducted using Microsoft Excel, with a focus on data exploration, customer segmentation, descriptive statistics, visualisation, and business interpretation.

## Business Questions

The analysis explores the following questions:

* How does churn vary across subscription types?
* How do contract lengths relate to customer churn?
* Is payment delay associated with higher churn?
* How do customer characteristics and spending patterns vary across segments?
* What potential customer-retention actions could be investigated based on the findings?

## Key Findings

The analysis identified differences in observed churn rates across customer segments.

One notable pattern was the association between payment delays and churn.

In the dataset, the observed churn rate was approximately 10% among customers with payment delays of up to 15 days, compared with approximately 66% among customers with delays of 16–23 days.

These results indicate a relationship worth investigating further. They do not establish that payment delays cause churn.

Additional findings and supporting calculations are available in the Excel workbook.

## Business Implications

The observed patterns suggest several areas for further investigation:

* Examine whether customers experiencing payment difficulties require additional support.
* Investigate why churn differs across contract lengths and subscription segments.
* Evaluate customer-retention initiatives using clearly defined performance metrics.
* Test potential interventions before concluding that they will improve retention.

These are potential business actions, not proven outcomes of the analysis.

## Limitations

* The project uses a provided dataset and guided project framework.
* The findings are based on the available variables and observations.
* Observational relationships do not establish causation.
* The analysis does not constitute a validated predictive churn model.
* Business recommendations require further validation before implementation.
